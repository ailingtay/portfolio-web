03 Experiment / Learn from the misses

What if it changes
the wrong thing?

The owl made the problem visible. A generation could add convincing movement—and change the scene I wanted to keep. Each miss gave me a more precise direction to test.

The first generation
wasn’t the last.

Each review led to a more specific direction: protect the framing, preserve the objects, reduce the movement.

04 Experiment / Rethink the tool

Making faster.
But making what?

The motion tests were one line of enquiry. Alongside them, I tested the repetitive setup: first a browser generator, then a tool inside After Effects. The useful discovery was what not to automate.

Test 01 / Browser prototype

It worked.
I missed the craft.

Using Claude, I built a browser tool that generated and exported a 16:9 timer. Setup was quick, but typography, animation and the finish felt too constrained.

This recording is unavailable. The process and stills remain visible below.

What I learnedAutomating the whole workflow wasn’t the goal.

Test 02 / After Effects tool

Build the structure.
Keep the freedom.

I moved the automation into After Effects. My tool creates the timer structure, while typography, colour, animation and effects stay editable.

This recording is unavailable. The process and stills remain visible below.

What changedLess repeated setup. More control over the result.

The shiftAutomate the setup. Keep the creative decisions editable.

05 Think beyond the short clip

What happens
after the clip ends?

A good motion test lasts seconds. A classroom timer can run for minutes. Rather than generate the entire duration, I treated the clips as pieces I could arrange.
